"Spy Vs. Spy" was the first thing i read in MAD Magazine. My 
father was a big fan of MAD and had a pile of them in his closet. 
When my brother and 1 discovered them, back around 1963, 1 was 
only three years old. Since I couldn't read at the time, I looked for 
the features without words. Antonio Prohias’ and Sergio Aragones’ 
art always made the magazine worth thumbing through. As | got 
older, I would go back and read the same issues and found more 
and more to enjoy as I learned about the world 


In 1975, I was 15, and my family took a trip cross-country from 
Calitornia to New York. While in New York City, my brother Bob 
and I begged our parents to let us visit the offices of MAD 
Magazine. My father agreed, and circled the block for a half hour 
while we ran up to the 13th floor of the Madison Avenue offices. 
When we arrived, the secretary asked if we had an appointment. 
Oh no! We had come so close and now it looked like we wouldn't 
get to see anything. We explained we had come all the way from 
California and this was our only chance to visit. Just then, the 
door opened, and MAD Editor Al Feldstein smiled and welcomed 
us inside. Wow, we're in! As we walked down the art-covered hall to 
the large layout/art room, we saw Leonard Brenner pasting 6 
down on some art. Al had given each of us a “cheap crummy sou- 
venir” — a mini poster of the whole staff, which we excitedly got 
everyone there to autograph for us.

Antonio Prohias was penciling one of his “Spy Vs. Spy" features. 
When we asked him for an autograph, he smiled, looking up from 
his art table, and asked “Who am 1% 1 said “Al Jaffee?” “No,” he said, 
“ am Antonio Prohias!" And with that, he took the souvenir sheet 
with all the printed sel&characters of the artists on it, and proceeded 
to show us who he was. He drew the Black and White Spies with their 
noses tied together in a knot. Then he asked my name, and wrote “To 
Tom Anderson, MADly —A. Prohias." I thanked him while shaking 
his hand. I was so happy to meet the man who drew something I had 
enjoyed for all those years. What a thrill it was. 


As the years went by, I would still get every issue of MAD. One day 
I noticed that Antonio no longer drew “Spy Vs. Spy.” Then I learned 
that he was in poor health in Miami. In 1997, I tracked down his 
address and wrote him a nice letter reminding him of the 1975 
visit to MAD and how much I had enjoyed “Spy Vs. Spy” all those 
years. A few months later, his daughter Marta wrote me for her 
father, expressing thanks for the letter and that it made him happy. 
I was sad when I learned he died. I remember him fondly and with 
admiration. He once took the time for me, making me very happy, 
and now I take the time to let others know what a truly kind and 
talented man Antonio Prohias was. 


Tom Anderson 
Birmingham, AL
