FAMILY UN-DYNAMIC 


Iam writing to you (grudgingly) to ask for a favor. You see, I am the 
fourth Joseph Salvatore Cannizzaro, my dad is the third, etc. Over the 
years, all the Joes have enjoyed MAD, from my grandpa in the'50s, my 
dad in the’70s, and me in the 2000s. And apart from being an angry 
family of Sicilians (not Italians!) we don’t share much except the unfor- 
tunate love of MAD. Now, besides leaving two generations of screw-ups, 
my grandparents haven't left a huge impact on the world. So I come to 
you today to ask you to please publish this letter to show that our reading 
of MAD for the better (worse) of five decades hasn’t been in vain (it 
probably has). My grandparents may never see the Cubs win the World 
Series, but they may see my letter get published in MAD Magazine.

GOON WITH THE "IND" 
I noticed in issue #540, (with Trump) you put the "IND" 
right by the "М" (in MAD), making it spell "MIND." Then 
I noticed, if you put "MAD" right by it, you get "MAD 


| MIND,” as in "Donald Trump has а mad mind"! Was this 


on purpose? 


| Eli Poll * Seattle, WA 


Poll Position — You've cracked what is perhaps MAD's longest- 
running joke! In 1958, the eerily prescient editors first put "IND" 
by the "M" in "MAD," on the cover of MAD #48. Knowing that 

it was a brilliant seed of a joke and we just needed to keep 
watering it, we put "IND" on nearly every cover for the next 59 
years. And finally, it paid off! Donald Trump was on our cover and 
our stinging comment was made! Sure, it was the second time 
he was on the cover of MAD, and we'll continue to put "IND" on 
every cover in the future, but you nailed it — #540 is the reason 
it's there! Well done, Eli — you've made sense of a 59-year-old 
joke! In fact, it's topped only by Trump himself: a 70-year-old 
joke that no one can make any sense of! —Ed.

REMEMBERING JACK DAVIS, 1924-2016 


It is with great sadness that we report that legendary MAD artist 
Jack Davis passed away on July 27 at the age of 91. 


Jack was опе of the founding members of MAD Magazine's "Usual 
Gang of Idiots." An enormously gifted and versatile artist, Davis' work 
appeared in the very first issue of MAD and virtually every issue over 
the next four decades. 


A list of Jack's legendary pieces would run several pages in length. His 
most iconic parodies from MAD's comic book days are of The Lone 
Ranger and High Noon. From the magazine, his notable parodies include 
spoofs of Raiders of the Lost Ark, Gone with the Wind, and M*A*S*H. 


Jack's final cover for MAD was issue #559 
(September 1995) and depicted Howard 
Stern being plunged into a toilet bowl Бу 
Alfred E. Neuman. The cover hangs framed 
in Stern's office and remains a MAD classic. 


Everyone at MAD and DC Entertainment 
send their heartfelt condolences to Jack's 
wife, Dena, and the entire Davis family. 


I was deeply saddened by the passing of my favorite artist, Jack 
Davis. Before MAD, I enjoyed his work in the earlier EC titles 
like Two-Fisted Tales, and, of course, Tales from the Crypt. І col- 
lected his magazine covers from Time and TV Guide and many 
other works I spotted throughout the decades. RIP Jack Davis.
