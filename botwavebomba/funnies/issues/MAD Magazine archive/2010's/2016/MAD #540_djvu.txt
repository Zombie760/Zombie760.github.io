wrongs. (But a word to all of you filthy Minnesotans out there: our prohibition 
still stands!) —Ed.

Wil-son of a Gun —Even though you dismiss Everybody Loves Raymond as

where it belongs! —Ed.

INTO THIN ERR 


I have enclosed a picture of me at Everest Base camp 
holding a copy of MAD. While trekking to Everest 
Base Camp, I noticed something very odd. The higher 
the altitude, the funnier the magazine became! By the 
time I got to base camp, I was laughing hysterically 
and thought MAD was the best magazine ever! I excit- 
edly called my wife on a satellite phone and told her to 
renew my subscription immediately. Alas, when I re- 
turned to sea level, I discovered that MAD was no lon- 
ger funny. 1 finally realized that the lack of oxygen to 
my brain at the high altitude was the reason I thought 
MAD was funny. Since I don't live in the mountains, 
can I cancel my subscription and get a refund? 


Bill Sullivan * Williamsville, NY 


Sulli-Van Wilder — Ah, Everest! Not many people know this, 
but about 60% of our subscriptions are the result of someone 
not getting enough oxygen to the brain — so we're quite 
familiar with "The Everest Effect"! That ol' pile of rocks has 
been VERY good to us over the years! We wish we could give 
you a refund, but given the rambling, childlike tone of your letter, your brain is clearly still working 
in very diminished capacity (even for a MAD reader). That cancellation would never hold up in court. 
Maybe you can get a Sherpa to help explain the jokes to you! Feel better and enjoy your sub! —Ed.

CORRECTIONS 


We make mistakes. A lot of them. We're not happy about them, 
and we're not happy to be writing about them (because, let's 
face it, we'll probably make more mistakes trying to correct the 
original mistakes). But correct them we must. Which is why 
we have to report that MAD #539 accidentally included an 
installment of “The Darker Side of...The Lighter Side of" that had 
previously appeared in MAD #527. Additionally, for “A MAD 
Look at Man Caves,” Tom Luth should have been credited as 
the colorist. We also mistakenly reported that John Caldwell 
passed away on February 26, when in fact it was February 21. 
We apologize for the errors.
