THE OPPOSABLE BABOONSBURY 
by Chee-Chee Trudeau 
Wickersham & Bros. Publishing, $18.95

Over the years, “Baboonsbury” has gotten a reputation as a partisan screed for 
the liberally-inclined chimpanzee. But that’s as lazy and simplistic as lumping the 
parvorder Platyrrhini together with the Catarrhinis, an obvious mistake no one 
would ever make. 

Besides, the divisiveness of current politics has reenergized Chee-Chee Trudeau's 
venerable strip. In recent years, it’s provided some of the sharpest, most incisive ape-related satire on the comics page. 

And like a mother macaque grooming parasites from the fur of its young, The Opposable Baboonsbury has carefully plucked 
the strongest storylines from the last eight years of strips. 

Anyone familiar with earlier Baboonsbury compilations such as Five Little Lobbyists Jumping on the Bed and Apple Cup Drink 
Social Security Reform will have an idea what to expect. Longtime fans have grown up with the rotating cast of characters, from 
Zonka the woolly capuchin to Uncle Dookie the drug-addicted lab monkey. 

For newcomers, it can be a bit much to absorb all in one gulp, the comic strip equivalent of trying to eat an entire termite colony 
in one meal. Don't be nervous, though. Start reading, and in no time at all, they'll seem like members of your very own phylum. 

These days, Trudeau primarily concentrates on the writing. Most of the art chores are handed over to a hyperactive pack of 
screeching pygmy marmosets. Even so, the artistic result is still a cut above most syndicated comic strips nowadays. 

As always, the storylines are a mix of the political and the personal. An “alpha male” sequence in which world leaders 
Curious George W., Jojo Blair and Vladimir Pooty-Poot try to one-up each other by flaunting the reddest, most dominant 
rump pad and anus is good for a few laughs. But only the full-color Sunday sequence truly brings the analogy home. 

More emotionally wrenching is the controversial series of strips featuring longtime character D.Z. A died-in-the-fur conserva- 
tive and an enthusiastic supporter of the war in Iraq, D.Z. volunteers for frontline service, only to get his prehensile tail blown 
off. His lengthy convalescence and recuperation struck a deep chord with readers. The Opposable Baboonsbury includes five 
heartfelt letters written by four badly-injured army veterans and one circus chimp who suffered a catastrophic unicycle accident. 

So, no matter where your political bias lies along the spectrum of animal-based comic strips there’s plenty to savor in 
“Baboonsbury.” On the aesthetic Banana Scale, it gets three and a half Chiquitas.
