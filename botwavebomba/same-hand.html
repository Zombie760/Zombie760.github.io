The pitch

An outlet's L/R lean tells you which team it cheers for. It does not tell you who pays the bill.

The financialized axis measures how heavily an outlet's coverage tilts toward capital interests — institutional money, asset managers, executive-pay framing, the dignity of markets. When an outlet rated centrist on AllSides also scores high on financialized, that's the tell. The colors of the jersey change. The owner of the league does not.

The chart below plots outlets that AllSides + MBFC rate across the L/R spectrum and that Bomba's editorial review scored at financialized ≥ 0.5. Note where the dots cluster.

What the dots tell you

Read the chart with one question: if left vs right were the real fight, why do the dots pile horizontally inside the puppet zone?

The L/R spread sells the tribal storyline. The vertical pile-up inside the puppet zone shows you what the storyline is selling for. An outlet at left and an outlet at right can both score financialized 0.7. They disagree on identity politics. They agree on capital.

Ground News literally cannot show you this picture. Their entire product is built on the X-axis. If they added the Y-axis, the product collapses.

Why Bomba can do this and Ground News can't

Bomba scores every outlet on six axes — western, adversarial, atlanticist, interventionist, statist, financialized — with a provenance trail on every dimension (which third-party rater, what date, what confidence). Ground News averages three raters into one number on one axis and calls it political-bias.

One axis is a tribe. Six axes are a fingerprint. The fingerprint shows the same hand.

Left or right doesn't matter. It's the same hand pulling every puppet string. They don't care who wins. They own the game.
