# The anti-ICE billionaire built his fortune on the cages

## The filing

99.9 percent of the money is his.

## The receipt

In 2005, Farallon Capital Management — the San Francisco hedge fund Tom Steyer co-founded in 1986 and ran for 26 years — held shares valued at **$89.1 million** in Corrections Corporation of America. Today that company is called CoreCivic. It runs private prisons. Two of them house people picked up by ICE.

Steyer left Farallon in 2012. He launched NextGen America in 2013. He spent $73 million on the 2014 election and roughly $100 million on 2016. By 2025 he was a climate-fund founder running an anti-ICE platform. By April 2026 he had spent more on his own gubernatorial primary than any candidate in the history of California.

Asked about the CoreCivic position, he called it a mistake. He said it was a wake-up call that he was in the wrong business. He did not say when, exactly, the wake-up happened. The shares were valued at $89.1 million in 2005. He stayed at Farallon for seven more years. The fund kept investing.

## The contradiction

Rep. Katie Porter said it cleanest: *If they're criminals, does that make the guy who invested $90M in their facilities an accessory?*

The class-traitor billionaire who wants to tax other billionaires built his fortune at a fund that put $89.1 million into the private prison company that contracts with ICE. The platform he's running on is the operating loss of the position he held.
